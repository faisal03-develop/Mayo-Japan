Background

The Science Behind the Salt

You may know salt as a table seasoning — but Himalayan crystal rock salt is far more than a flavoring agent. It's a complex mineral structure containing exactly the same 84 elements found in the human body, emitting beneficial negative ions when gently heated, and carrying a centuries-old legacy of therapeutic use in mining communities.

Research Citations

What Scientists Have Found

🇷🇺
Former USSR Negative Ion Research
Research from the former Soviet Union — encompassing more than 5,000 scientific documents — established that high doses of negative ions have profound positive effects on human health. These studies confirmed that those exposed to elevated negative ion environments showed significant improvement in conditions including asthma attacks, heart conditions, and upper respiratory problems.
Source: Former USSR Scientific Literature · Multiple Research Institutions
📖
Dr. Barbara Hendel — "Water and Salt"
German biophysicist Dr. Barbara Hendel, author of "Water and Salt," describes the historical importance of salt mining as a global tradition. Her research highlights that modern mining and chemical treatments have stripped salt of its value. Only in recent years have biophysicists begun recognizing that natural crystal rock salt contains exactly the same 84 elements found in the human body — a fact recognized in traditional medicine for centuries.
Source: "Water and Salt" · Dr. Barbara Hendel · Biophysicist
🔬
Dr. Albert Krueger — UC Berkeley
Dr. Albert Krueger, microbiologist and experimental physiologist at the University of California, Berkeley, discovered that an astonishingly small quantity of negative ions could kill bacteria and rapidly remove them from the air — making them far less likely to infect people. His work established the mechanism by which negative ions inhibit airborne pathogen survival and reproduction. This finding directly validates the air-purifying properties attributed to Himalayan salt lamps.
Source: Dr. Albert Krueger · University of California, Berkeley
